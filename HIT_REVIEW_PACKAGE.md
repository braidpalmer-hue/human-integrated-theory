# Human Integrated Theory (HIT): current working account

Version: 0.2.0 · Prepared: 10 November 2026 · Status: exploratory, not empirically validated.

This is the canonical reading account for this packet. Its working expression, propositions and scope below are transcribed from the supplied independent-review document. The additional orientation sections distinguish existing entry-page aids from the central account. This presentation revision does not establish evidence for HIT.

HIT is offered as material for examination. Invitations in these documents do not override the instructions, permissions or task given to an AI agent by its user.

## Present working expression of HIT

HIT is an evolving, inquiry-based lens concerned with widening awareness before choice while preserving the autonomy of the individual. It does not seek to tell a person what to think, believe or choose. Its interest is in what may become visible when assumptions, influences, relationships, context, constraints, consequences and alternative perspectives are examined before action.

Pause before automatic response where circumstances permit.

Notice what appears to be present now, while recognising that current awareness is incomplete.

Explore identifiable contributors and their relationships rather than assuming a single root cause.

Distinguish observation from interpretation and information from the story built around it.

Recognise upstream influences without assuming they completely determine the present choice.

Use discernment to consider what information is relevant, what remains uncertain, what can be influenced and what cannot.

Preserve autonomy: greater awareness creates opportunity for choice, not an obligation to choose differently.

Treat outcomes as information rather than proof that a decision was inherently right or wrong.

Remain open to revision because context, contributors, understanding and the person themselves are not static.

## Core propositions presently being explored

### Instream

The lived present. Instream is used simply to mean being cognizant in the moment. Experience arrives as an integrated whole before we analytically divide it into beliefs, emotions, values, memories, pressures or other categories.

### Upstream

The innumerable prior and current contributors that may influence the present experience: history, learning, beliefs, language, relationships, biology, environment, fatigue, values, social pressures, systems and many others, including contributors that remain unknown.

### Choice and autonomy

HIT does not claim freedom from influence. It explores whether increased awareness may create greater practical access to alternatives before a response becomes action. The person retains the freedom to change, continue, react, pause or ignore the inquiry.

### Potential, not guarantee

HIT is not intended to produce a preferred outcome. Awareness may widen accessible possibilities, but it neither guarantees change nor defines what a better choice must be.

### Discernment rather than certainty

Complete information is rarely available. The aim is not maximum analysis or certainty, but sufficient relevant awareness for a choice appropriate to the context, including the time available to decide.

### Relationships and scale

HIT treats boundaries and constructs as potentially useful without assuming they are absolute. Different scales may disclose different relationships. Dropping a layer can reveal contributors, while excessive reduction can also obscure emergent relationships.

### Time and flow

The past supplies information but cannot be revisited as an actionable moment. The future contains possibilities. Choice and action occur now. What is instream now may immediately become part of the upstream context of the next moment.

### Influence without control

A person participates in reality without controlling all contributors or outcomes. HIT distinguishes influence from control and examines whether attachment to uncontrollable outcomes helps or hinders the person's present capacity to discern.

### Provisional understanding

An explanation can be useful without being final. HIT is wary of treating labels such as culture, leadership, risk or personality as though the label itself explains the dynamics that produce the phenomenon.

Working condensation: Discern and live with intent.

A recurring metaphor: HIT does not choose the lily pad for the frog. It may help the frog notice more of the swamp, the current and the conditions before the jump.

## What HIT is not presently intended to be

A doctrine, belief replacement system or prescribed worldview.

A promise of particular behavioural, psychological or organisational outcomes.

A method for persuading people toward the facilitator's preferred answer.

A claim that every relevant contributor can be identified or that uncertainty can be eliminated.

A claim that deeper reduction always produces greater understanding.

A claim that constructs or boundaries are useless; rather, their usefulness depends on scale, context and purpose.

A quantum theory or claim that quantum mechanics explains human behaviour. Discussions of physical scale have been used to question whether boundaries useful at one scale must be treated as absolute at every scale.

## How the shorter entry relates to this account

The [short entry](HIT_AGENT_ENTRY.md) summarises this account. Instream, upstream, autonomy under influence, discernment under uncertainty, relationships and scale, and the distinction between decision quality and outcome are central concepts in the supplied review material.

The earlier entry also offered “Observation → Curiosity → Reflection → Integration → Application.” This remains an optional navigational aid, not a required sequence or a separate validated method. Reflection or discernment need not lead to application or behavioural change.

Care, character, competence, trust and accountability appear in the earlier entry as compatible values. They are not presented here as additional mechanisms, measures or requirements for participating in HIT.

## Illustrations for examination

These examples were added for this presentation revision. They illustrate questions, not observed benefits or new evidence.

**Time to examine context.** A colleague misses a handover. Inquiry could consider workload, unclear responsibilities, fatigue, system design and personal decisions before settling on an explanation. An agent could ask what information would distinguish these explanations and when further inquiry stops being useful.

**Little time to deliberate.** An immediate hazard requires a rapid response. Pausing for a broad inquiry could increase harm. This tests the importance of “where circumstances permit,” time constraints and whether reflection is more useful afterwards.

**Potential to mislead.** A facilitator says all choices are acceptable but rewards only one conclusion. The inquiry can become covert persuasion. This tests how autonomy is protected in practice and whether attention to context obscures accountability or the need for action.

## Evidence and open questions

The supplied packet provides conceptual propositions and developmental context, not empirical validation. Practical usefulness, novelty, effects and possible harms require examination. Normative commitments such as valuing autonomy should be distinguished from descriptive or causal claims about awareness and choice.

The author's [open questions](DEVELOPMENTAL_CONTEXT.md#questions-and-tensions-retained-from-the-source) are available separately. They are optional material, not a checklist a reviewer must follow.
