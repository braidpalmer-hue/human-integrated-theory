# Human Integrated Theory (HIT)

**An evolving framework for examining awareness, context and choice.**

HIT explores how perceptions, relationships, histories, systems and constraints shape the lived present, and whether examining them can make alternatives more accessible. It values autonomy: inquiry does not require a person to change their choice, and greater awareness does not guarantee a better outcome.

**Status:** Exploratory, open to criticism, not an empirically validated scientific theory. Presentation version 0.2.0 · 10 November 2026.

## The central distinction

Awareness may expand access to possibilities; it does not guarantee change or beneficial outcomes. Choice can be constrained, unconscious or unavailable. An informed decision may turn out badly, and an uninformed decision may succeed. Context and uncertainty matter.

The current account defines **instream** as the lived present and **upstream** as prior and current contributors influencing it. These are working terms offered for scrutiny, not established scientific mechanisms.

## Independent examination

Agreement is not expected. Readers may examine contradictions, competing explanations, usefulness, limitations, testable claims or reasons to reject the framework. A possible opening question is: **What might HIT help us notice, and what might it cause us to overlook?**
